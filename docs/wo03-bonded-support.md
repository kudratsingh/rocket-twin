# WO-03 candidate bonded load path: exploratory structural solve

**Disposition: open.** A conformal, three-material 3D static solve was executed on the copy of G54 Package A; it does not establish flight loads, qualified bond or shell strength, or WO-03 completion. The specified peak-stress mesh criterion fails. The fixture and equilibrium checks do not validate the material properties or joint construction. All material inputs here are provisional.

The input is the exact copy `build/wo03-correction-package-20261030T224434934669Z/G54-0.075/resolved-candidate.yaml` (SHA-256 `bf8aa2e15a15d04045bfe4c4f8bb6e0edb12dc1b77bbb27331141874da78129e`); the active spec was not changed. `scripts/wo03_bonded_support.py` regenerates the actual motor mount, five adhesive annuli and full slotted aft shell from this resolved copy, at their vehicle stations, exports STEP and imports/fragments them in Gmsh. Three of the five adhesive annuli comprise three solids each due to the actual slots. Each bond shares surfaces with both the mount and the shell; overlapping material fragments are rejected, and element material coverage is checked. The model has no artificial axial or radial ground restraints at intermediate rings. At the front shell joint (body station 922 mm), axial displacement is fixed, with three additional point constraints to remove in-plane rigid-body modes. These ideal joint conditions are not a demonstrated physical joint stiffness.

The applied load is the exploratory full 139.97 N at six actual longitudinal rail flanks, integrated over the finger engagement band body stations 1001–1019 mm (694.71 mm²). This is a static load-transfer sensitivity, **not** an accelerating-vehicle load case. It is not a demonstrated upper bound for every local stress. It does not include motor and vehicle inertia, aerodynamic loads, mass-growth location, hot-property redistribution, realistic joint compliance, or thermal expansion. In flight the effective endplate-flank load must account for motor inertia, and the shell-front reaction reflects the forward assembly acceleration after aft-component inertia; simply applying all thrust against a grounded front is not the flight load. The other aft components' distributed inertia and their actual attachment locations must be accounted for in a separate physical case.

## Accelerating-frame sensitivity, executed follow-up

A separate analysis uses the exact G54 curve hypothesis and the resolved copy's CAD-reconciled part masses. At the largest sampled thrust, t=0.135 s, the 1.15-scaled thrust is 139.9688 N. Impulse-proportional burn depletion leaves 0.189707 kg of motor; total mass including 0.050 kg growth is 0.878696 kg. The axial specific acceleration is `T/m = 159.2915 m/s²`; using this specific acceleration includes the gravity term in the accelerating frame and does not apply a second design factor. The net motor/endplate flank load is `T − m_motor T/m = 109.7502 N`. The same sample is maximal among the curve knots for both specific acceleration and this flank load. Drag, off-axis acceleration, powered acoustic loads and between-sample transients are not included.

Two growth placements bracket an explicitly assigned extra 50 g at an upper assembly location (forward of the 0.922 m joint) or an aft shell patch centered at 1.100 m. The modeled solid parts receive inertia distributed by their mesh element volumes and reconciled CAD mass; other aft components receive inertia on named mount or shell attachment patches, at their estimated CG stations. Those patch attachments approximate the real load path and can cause mesh-dependent local peaks. Front expected reactions from mass balance are 58.80048 N and 50.83590 N, respectively. No thrust load is applied directly to the front joint. The 4 mm CalculiX nodal-force print reports 58.81943 N and 50.85486 N; these include 0.01895 N of applied shell inertia on constrained front nodes. Subtracting that directly applied constrained-node load gives the above net reactions. The raw `equilibrium_residual_n` in these historical reports does not make this correction; it should not be interpreted as an actual force imbalance.

The six 4 mm cases use cold (plywood/cardboard/epoxy E = 2000/500/2000 MPa), hot nominal (1000/250/2000 MPa), and hot soft bond (1000/250/100 MPa) for each growth location. These are elastic property sensitivities; no sourcing, long-term stiffness, cure, failure or thermal expansion qualification is implied. Peak strengths below are un-converged, include edge and attachment singularities, and are retained rather than excluded. DF=3 is applied once when comparing each stress to its provisional strength.

For example, forward/hot-nominal epoxy shear gives 3 × 1.476 = 4.43 MPa against a provisional 1 MPa hot shear strength: the raw-peak screen fails. Forward/hot-nominal paper gives 3 × 1.088 = 3.26 MPa against provisional 2 MPa; the local raw-peak screen fails. These failures are conservative screens, not proof of physical failure, because both the load attachment patches and high-gradient stresses need validation. Soft-epoxy peak shear is close to the provisional DF3 threshold at this mesh and is not a pass. No allowable for radial peel or combined peel/shear is established.

The forward-growth 4 mm shell's fixed 940–980 mm region has signed volume-weighted axial stress −0.5847 MPa (estimated section force −60.15 N using unslotted gross area). The additional magnitude relative to the front reaction is consistent with intervening shell inertia; direct cross-section integration is still needed. The signed mid-axial-band bond averages are recorded per ring in the run report; signed averaging can cancel local stresses, so these do not waive the raw peaks or substitute for an area-integrated mid-gap traction check. A five-ring nominal aggregate shear-spring stiffness of 4.37×10⁶ N/mm (or 2.19×10⁵ N/mm for the 100 MPa epoxy sensitivity) is far above the unslotted shell's 51,445 N axial EA divided by a 187 mm span, 275 N/mm; this supports checking shell compliance and localized transfer rather than treating each ring as ground. These are comparisons of simplified stiffnesses, not a resolved joint stiffness. At nominal area and 0.5 mm bond gap, thermal conduction alone would be approximately 0.85–1.42 W/K for conductivity 0.15–0.25 W/(m K); wetting, real contact resistance, heat capacity, and the 0.32–0.68 mm gap bounds remain unresolved.

A possible design direction is a lightweight, mechanically continuous shell reinforcement and properly detailed bond fillets around the ring stations, with finite overlap and explicit load transfer into the forward joint. Its material, geometry, mass, shell contact, end details, and hot properties must be specified and analyzed; no increased allowable is credited here. A local band alone does not necessarily reduce the forward shell's continuous axial load or fix joint crushing.

The 3 mm forward/hot-nominal result gives displacement 0.5189 mm versus 0.5029 mm at 4 mm (+3.2%). Paper peak |axial| increases from 1.088 to 1.178 MPa (+8.3%); DF3 gives 3.53 MPa against estimated 2 MPa. Epoxy peak axial-radial shear drops from 1.476 to 0.978 MPa (−33.7%), so peak-stress convergence fails and DF3 demand 2.93 MPa exceeds the provisional 1 MPa hot shear strength. Plywood maximum von Mises increases from 1.353 to 2.140 MPa (+58%); DF3 demand 6.42 MPa exceeds provisional 5 MPa hot strength. The soft-bond 3 mm peak shear is 0.324 MPa versus 0.332 MPa at 4 mm (−2.4%); DF3 gives 0.972 MPa versus 1 MPa, but this narrow isolated result does not qualify the joint. All local peaks remain sensitive to idealized attachment patches, geometric edges, and unmeasured properties; none establish a physically qualified failure load.

The current thermal engineering input estimates epoxy heat capacity at 1,200 J/(kg K); applied to the CAD-reconciled five-bond mass of approximately 0.001704 kg, that is approximately 2.04 J/K total. This is an estimate, not a measured supplier-specific heat capacity. The assumed geometry and nominal conductivity are shared with the mechanical hand check; no perfect-wetting thermal contact claim follows from the mechanically tied mesh.

The 3 mm sweep completed all six combinations in `build/wo03-bonded-physical-3/report.json` with the same frozen source hashes as its own run; the 4 mm sweep is `build/wo03-bonded-physical-4/report.json`. At 4 to 3 mm, the fixed forward-shell signed mean changes from −0.58466 to −0.5845 MPa, while the forward/hot-nominal first bond's signed 1 mm mid-band shear mean changes 0.05088 to 0.07077 MPa (+39%); the latter fails the 10% regional-stress benchmark. The linearized tetrahedral volume weighting and element-centroid region selection are approximations, and the first-bond estimate is not a proper conformal mid-gap surface traction resultant. The source now also reports regional maximum and volume-weighted mean absolute stresses and corrects the restrained-node force accounting; these changes do not retroactively alter the two recorded reports.

The corrected single-case forward/hot-nominal reruns at `build/wo03-bonded-physical-corrected-{5,4,3}` use the final script and confirm unchanged input hashes. They give net front reaction 58.800475 N with absolute equilibrium residual below 5×10⁻⁷ N. The 5/4/3 mm displacement sequence is 0.44314/0.50290/0.51892 mm. In the first bond's fixed central band, the volume-weighted mean absolute radial-axial shear is 0.07688/0.07646/0.08439 MPa: the 4-to-3 change is +10.36%, narrowly exceeding the 10% benchmark. Its regional peak is 1.08585/1.01620/0.67586 MPa, a 33.5% decrease from 4 to 3 mm, so peak convergence also fails. The fixed forward-shell band peak absolute axial stress is 0.84830/0.81802/0.81041 MPa; the 4-to-3 change is 0.93%, and 3 × 0.81041 = 2.43 MPa exceeds the estimated 2 MPa paper strength. This is a failed provisional screen, not physical qualification or a measured failure; a continuous axial reinforcement and its load transfer into the joint merit study. The regional averages and maxima use element-centroid inclusion and linearized tetrahedron volumes rather than an exact cut-surface integration, and the local material/attachment uncertainty remains open.

## Candidate PU and external strip trade

This copy-only trade regenerates every part from the historical resolved G54 candidate after clearing cached CAD masses, then reconciles current geometry and records a new CAD-source identity and part STEP hashes. It does not alter the active configuration. The regenerated CAD manifest is stored with each run. Geometry is still the five-ring, 0.5 mm nominal mount-shell gap; the adhesive alternative and strip solids are study-only candidates.

Candidate A replaces the annulus elastic input with **assumed** PU Young's modulus 2–20 MPa. [Sika's Sikaflex-252 data sheet](https://industry.sika.com/content/dam/dms/us01/o/sikaflex-252.pdf) reports 1.20 kg/L uncured density, 3 MPa tensile strength, 2.5 MPa lap shear, 400% elongation and 50 Shore A hardness under its stated laboratory conditions; it does **not** supply the 2–20 MPa elastic range used here. Moisture cure, joint accessibility, creep, shrinkage, surface treatment, wetting and compatibility of the particular timber/paper/carbon substrates and 0.5 mm bondline need evidence. The analysis does not promote typical supplier values into design allowables: it keeps the previous provisional 1 MPa hot shear screen and 2 MPa paper screen. Existing estimated thermal k=0.15–0.25 W/(m K) and cp=1200 J/(kg K) are retained only as unverified placeholders for this different material; they must be revalidated.

Candidate B adds three continuous outside strips between fins at 60°, 180°, 300°, each a curved sector 3 mm contact arc × 0.5 mm radial thickness and 278 mm length, with a conformal 0.5 mm PU film from shell R33 to R33.5 mm and carbon from R33.5 to R34 mm. The resulting nominal combined added mass is 3.30 g (including film). These are untapered study solids and not an adopted part. The solve directly restrains the strip and adhesive front ends at 922 mm together with the shell: this ideal projection assumes full load transfer into the forward joint and does not model an overlap onto the coupler, taper/fillet or peel there. Such an overlap and its shell/coupler reinforcement require explicit geometry, finite joint stiffness, strength and execution. The study is for this fixed-mode G54 copy only.

[Easy Composites' pultrusion data sheet](https://media.easycomposites.co.uk/datasheets/EC-TDS-Carbon-Fibre-Pultrusions.pdf) reports typical lengthwise tensile modulus 28–40 GPa and lengthwise compressive modulus 10–20 GPa, plus typical lengthwise compressive strength 200–320 MPa and 80 °C maximum service temperature. It warns that values are typical, not specifications. The hot 14 GPa axial modulus and 200 MPa compressive strength used here are engineering sensitivities, not supplier-proven hot values. The current 3D element material is an **isotropic** approximation with E=14 GPa, not a validated anisotropic pultrusion; transverse, shear, peel and local buckling predictions are correspondingly uncertain.

The first 4 mm trial (before independent adhesive-region reporting) gave, for A vs B respectively, 0.488 vs 0.388 mm displacement, peak paper |axial stress| 0.981 vs 0.617 MPa, and fixed forward-shell band peak 0.807 vs 0.432 MPa. With DF3 the paper peak demands 2.94 vs 1.85 MPa against the provisional 2 MPa screen. A's mount adhesive shear peak was 0.214 MPa, while B's combined ring and strip-bond shear peak was 0.159 MPa. B's maximum carbon |axial stress| was 9.195 MPa. These are single-mesh values under the ideal projected front support and are not qualification. A's paper screen fails; B is a promising but unqualified candidate. The final study separates ring adhesive and strip-film stresses; its three-level and modulus-sensitivity results supersede these initial outputs.
