# WO-03 experimental G80 fin planforms

**Three inspectable study copies; none is adopted or flight-ready.** Two alternative swept fin shapes are compared with the current G80 rectangular fins using the existing native fin generator, current source materials and the existing mass/Barrowman backend. This is a geometry and static-stability trade, not evidence of reduced drag, higher apogee, root strength or flutter resistance. Trainer and finless configurations are not changed.

Run `.venv/bin/python scripts/wo03_aerodynamic_fin_trade.py --config configs/studies/wo03-aerodynamic-fin-trade.yaml --preset tiny`. The ignored [candidate index](../build/wo03-aerodynamic-fin-trade/latest.json) identifies each resolved YAML, CAD manifest, spec and generator hashes, fin and assembly GLB hashes, and marks every entry `experimental`, `accepted: false`. Each candidate directory includes the full resolved YAML, full-vehicle STEP and GLB assemblies, per-part STEP/STL/GLB, a manifest and a numerical comparison. Source hashes were unchanged during this run. These artifacts are local study outputs, not active-configuration Analysis overlays or authoritative engineering results.

The three fins retain 120 mm root chord, front station 1.080 m, 6.35 mm nominal aircraft plywood, 0.3 mm thickness tolerance, three 100 × 2.5 mm tabs and the existing root gusset parameters. The 6.35 mm material and 715 kg/m³ density are unchanged; all masses below are design values derived from CAD volume, not physical measurements. The two new geometries are proposed dimensions, not a claim of a globally optimum planform. NASA's [model-rocket fin guidance](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/sl_video_instruction_book.pdf) describes tapered and cropped-delta options and discusses taper ratio as one design variable; it does not validate these dimensions. Normal-force buildup and CP use the repository's small-angle subsonic implementation of [Barrowman's method](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20010047838), not a CFD or flight measurement.

The static margins put the entire existing 50 g design allowance at the aft vehicle end, separately at loaded and burnout motor states. No mass allowance was removed. Loaded CG stations are respectively 0.79281, 0.78977 and 0.78725 m; the records contain the complete loaded/burnout inertia tensors, per-part mass/geometry, calculation inputs and all identities. The aft CP shift and increased margin are calculated by the stated model, not a claim that greater static margin is necessarily optimal for handling, weathercocking or performance.

Root ends are at 1.200 m; tip trailing edges lie at 1.200, 1.190 and 1.195 m respectively, within the 1.212 m vehicle body-length envelope. The tab parameters are unchanged and exact rigid-intersection checks of `fin_set` against the other current nominal solids found no overlap above the existing 0.05 mm³ threshold. **That check is not full fit verification:** it excludes soft items and says nothing about tolerance, print/cut allowance, adhesive, access, deflection or launch guide clearance. All generated fin solids pass the native validity check and all exported configurations pass the CAD/spec design-mass reconciliation.

## What the current CAD can and cannot show

The existing gusset generator extrudes the triangular root feet over the full root chord, assuming the original rectangular panel. To inspect the actual solids, the study subtracts a matching no-gusset panel from the generated fin, then compares the remainder against the panel's planform projection with a 1 mm root extension to include the curved body foot. The rectangular reference has essentially **0 mm³** outside that projection; the swept tapered and cropped-delta candidates have **279.46 mm³** and **423.90 mm³** respectively. Those volumes are across the complete three-fin assembly. They expose protruding root-gusset geometry, not an intentional aerodynamic fillet; a real swept gusset must be redesigned before the candidate can be treated as manufacturable. The projection is a geometric diagnostic, not an analysis of load transfer or material thickness.

The existing equivalent-thickness shell fin analysis explicitly rejects the swept root-gusset profile. Neither alternative has an installed-root joint, minimum-thickness strength, convergence or modal/flutter pass. Rework the gusset CAD and analysis representation, then execute the required structural and vibration/flutter screens on the complete chosen assembly. In particular, nominal zero intersection or a higher calculated static margin does not qualify the fins.

The exported fins also have the native **square leading and trailing edges**. A rounded leading edge and beveled trailing edge are potential future custom features requiring specified geometry, remaining minimum thickness, manufacture and aerodynamic/structural validation. No bevel is hidden in a display mesh. The existing JAX empirical drag-coefficient table is identical for the three candidates and is not geometry-sensitive; a flight run with it cannot support a physical fin-drag or altitude ranking. NASA notes that [rocket drag depends on geometry and operating conditions](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/rocket-aerodynamics/); quantitative drag and altitude differences are therefore **unavailable** pending a justified geometry-sensitive model or measurement. No arbitrary coefficient reduction is applied.

Candidate integration into the inspector, decisions about the preferred shape, unchanged full-scale engineering gates and future owner-performed construction and tests remain separate. No hardware, purchase, paid run, active design change or release is authorized by this study.
