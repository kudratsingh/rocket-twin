## Pressure seal sensitivity

The direct-aft recovery tether would change the old upper G11 bulkhead's function to pressure separation and local support, subject to real independent retention and a sealed tether passage. At the current CAD geometry/density, 3.0 mm nominal gives 18.140 g (12.093 g saving) and 3.5 mm gives 21.164 g (9.070 g saving), relative to the existing 30.234 g disk. Neither is adopted. A separate upper-joint collar is explicitly charged at 6 or 10 g, rather than treating reinforcement as free. For 5 mm fins, 3.0 mm bulkhead and 6 g collar give 52.647 g of modeled recovery headroom—only 0.479 g above the 52.168 g hypothesis—while a 10 g collar gives 48.647 g and fails. A 3.5 mm seal with a 6 g collar gives 49.624 g and fails. These arithmetic results remain conditional on the same guide, unchanged 50 g allowance and real rail saving.

The executed bounded calculation is also recorded in `build/wo03-trainer-fin-package/pressure-seal-record.json`. Pressure calculations assume a **25 kPa service differential**, 50 kPa proof point and 3 × 25 kPa factored service demand. These are proposed bounded conditions, not measurements, an ejection-charge rating or an assertion about actual venting; they must be shown to cover the selected system. Using an ideal simply supported 64.6 mm diameter circular plate, 2.7 mm minimum thickness (0.3 mm tolerance), estimated 10 GPa modulus and Poisson ratio 0.3 gives at 25/50 kPa a 81.94/163.88 N resultant, 1.48/2.95 MPa peak plate-bending estimate, and 0.096/0.192 mm center deflection. Factored 75 kPa gives 4.43 MPa estimated bending against the existing 25 MPa generic hot estimate. A hypothesized fully bonded, 1.5 mm axial perimeter contact gives 0.81 MPa mean bond shear at 75 kPa against the existing 1 MPa hot estimate. These are idealized screens: the central tether port and seal, discontinuous support, edge bending and peel, kraft wall/attachment, charge transients and actual proof must be designed and measured before either pressure or mass result can be accepted.

## Fixed-aft recovery load path remains open

The separate direct-aft recovery prototype is an explicit mass hypothesis here, not a proven complete load path. At the factored G54 late-delay probe, its aft anchor carries approximately 1,571 N; subtracting approximately 122 N of fore-plate inertia leaves approximately **1,449 N** to reach the aft structure. These forces already include the project factor of three. Three clipped plywood rails at approximately 44 mm² each and an estimated hot axial screen of 5 MPa have a combined nominal screen of only **660 N**, leaving an approximately **789 N** deficit if the entire transfer uses those rails. A 0.93 MPa average finger-bond shear below the 1 MPa estimated bond screen therefore does not establish the complete path. Load-sharing and rail material/contact are not verified.

The G54 motor alone contributes approximately 536 N factored reverse inertia, while roughly 913 N of the simplified post-plate transfer represents the rest of the tethered frame/body/fin assembly. The motor-only reverse number may be below the 660 N combined-rail screen, but its actual retainer, contact and load-sharing path still need analysis. The remaining loads require explicit alternate branches or demonstrated shell/frame transfer; the old keyed joint does not supply rated axial retention. This recovery case is distinct from a 140 N peak-thrust input multiplied by three; the two load definitions must not be combined or factored twice. Analysis and correction are open WO-03 work, not merely a later physical-proof item.

### Quantified acceptance gaps

The selected force is the larger of the retained 40 kPa/5° panel screen (**49.807 N**) and **CN=2 at 3,430.302 Pa**, the maximum q over all five matching historical trainer scenarios. The prior 31.846 N sensitivity used a narrower nominal case. These historical loads are explicitly transferred bounds: changed mass, fin station, frame channels and laminate require a fresh same-candidate flight/reference/uncertainty campaign.

At **0.12 mm minimum skins**, an assumed **4.7 mm minimum core**, and **50%** hot stiffness/strength, the piecewise uniform-pressure bending/core-shear calculation gives **0.4672 + 0.1392 = 0.6064 mm** panel deflection. It takes no gusset stiffening credit. Root rotation alone would require **Kθ ≥ 358.7 N·m/rad** if root translation were zero; translation consumes the same 0.3936 mm budget. The foam shear demand is **0.0730 MPa**, with a **+1.740** DF3 margin against the derated typical value. The maximum face stress requires at least **69.76 MPa hot tension and compression strength including DF3**. Typical tensile data alone do not establish compression, face wrinkling or microbuckling. Hot fractions, core tolerance, glass and shear/bond properties remain estimates; the **45°C substrate screen is unchanged**.

The two physical spanwise stress regions are fixed at **0–20 mm solid root** and **20–70 mm foam panel**. The unit-load integration matches the independent homogeneous uniform-cantilever bending/shear fixture; 16/32/64-point integration changes final deflection by approximately **5.6e-16 relative**. This verifies this reduction and integration only. It calculates no chordwise normal stress, in-plane span/chord shear or local cap/root/bond-edge stress, and supplies no replacement FEA convergence pass. The earlier 3.5→2.5 mm failures (**10.411% Syy / 26.797% shear**) and 2.5→2.0 mm failures (**22.909% / 14.406%**) remain failures under the same strict 10% criterion, with original report hashes retained.

Actual gussets are wedges extruded over the chord: their fin-face bond is **2,400 mm² per side**, while the curved body foot is only approximately **355 mm² per side**. The former attachment screen's triangular-area proxy and use of the radial gusset span as the side-to-side couple lever do not represent those interfaces. Integrating the actual foot's curved normals/tangents with a finite hot adhesive spring balances the force and root moment. Even with a rigid root and rigid cylindrical backing, it requires **2.450 MPa hot normal/peel capacity including DF3**, versus **0.537 MPa** in shear. A 1 MPa shear estimate cannot pass the normal/peel demand. Its **1.10–3.30 μm** added tip-motion range is an optimistic adhesive-only sensitivity, with no installed lower stiffness bound. No singular peak is clipped or discarded; the smooth spring model does not resolve bond-edge singularities. Paper through-thickness strength, substrate flexibility, frame/ring deformation, local peel and all assembled load sharing remain critical open methods.

A closed-cap torsion sensitivity gives an installed static-divergence stiffness target of **2.114 N·m/rad** at 1.2² times historical maximum q, assuming a destabilizing quarter-chord eccentricity. The estimated laminate/root-band model would require a further root torsional stiffness of **2.53–2.83 N·m/rad** in series. Skin shear modulus, cap transfer and installed torsion/damping are unverified. This is a necessary static stiffness target; it is **not flutter clearance**. Homogeneous NACA TN 4197 thickness substitution is inapplicable to this layered sandwich; a dynamic bending/torsion/body/TVC aeroelastic calculation and installed modal/torsion measurements remain required.
