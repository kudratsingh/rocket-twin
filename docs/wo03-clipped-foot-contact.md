# G12 slot-clipped foot contact sensitivity

**Clipping the ideal foot foundation to the nominal shell-backed area increases
the modeled hot normal demand.** For the 0.1 mm foot-bond case, the smooth
DF3 foot-normal demand rises from **2.357 to 2.648 MPa** (+12.37%), while the
foot's tangential force changes from 54.386 to 50.404 N and the tab's from
3.243 to 7.225 N. These are a reduced-model sensitivity with rigid root and
rigid supports. They are not an installed strength pass or a conservative bound
on the complete three-dimensional joint.

## Geometry and identity

The source is the exact historical G12 layered-fin/hoop resolved identity
`4ff75b8f…`, not the G54 candidate. The first fin has R=33 mm, root
z=1.055–1.175 m, and each foot occupies the recorded absolute tangential range
2.920–5.8518566 mm on the cylindrical body. The shell slot is 7.3 mm wide,
z=1.065–1.165 m: each end leaves a 10 mm axial strip with the full foot arc,
and the middle 100 mm leaves only the part beyond 3.650 mm from the slot center.
The analytical area per foot is

`R [ (L−Ls)(asin(y1/R)−asin(y0/R)) + Ls(asin(y1/R)−asin(w/(2R))) ]`.

It gives **281.7313444918093 mm²** per foot. The independently executed BRep
face intersections gave 281.7313444918701 and 281.7313444934131 mm², with
maximum discrepancy below 2e-9 mm², well inside the unchanged 1e-6 mm² area
tolerance. The full shell-facing foot area was 355.097301 mm². This support
mask follows the recorded slot dimensions and the actual common-area result;
it does not move a face or fill a gap. The shell is generated by current source;
its exact historical BRep correspondence is not established. See
[contact-face audit](wo03-root-contact-faces.md).

## Calculation and comparison

The existing reduced model uses shared root DOFs `[uX,uY,thetaZ]`, one
finite-spring normal/shear operator per curved foot and the previously recorded
three finite tab-face springs. The same estimated hot adhesive modulus **1 GPa**,
Poisson ratio **0.3**, 57.629073 N service force and 2.017018 N·m axial-Z root
moment are retained. The shell-backed patches use separate angular integration
on the full end strips and the slot-clipped middle strip; omitted springs do
not represent a free-standing adhesive bridge or a resolved free edge. The
unchanged tab spring comes from the earlier finite-face proxy. Its planar
side columns stop at x=31.791155 mm, where the cylindrical frame cuts the
opposing planar channel wall at the 3.65 mm outer potting edge. The later
root-to-potting BRep contact continues to x=31.902194 mm at the 2.5 mm tab
edge, but that extra corner does not have a full 1.15 mm opposed planar
column. Thus the unequal areas alone do not prove the earlier proxy wrong: it
models paired planar spring overlap while omitting the curved corner and its
coupled potting behavior. This calculation isolates the foot-mask change rather
than revising every interface or establishing total joint capacity.

Forces and moments in the table are service resultants; DF3 is applied once to
the peak traction demands only. The smooth normal peaks use absolute magnitude;
they are not resolved bond-edge singularities or qualified peel capacities.
Even the nominal tab proxy, ideal root, rigid paper/frame and full wetting within
the supported mask are assumptions. Real body, frame, ring and joint compliance,
three-dimensional load transfer, adhesive bridging and edge stresses are not
included. Removing grounded springs is a sensitivity, **not** a proved
conservative three-dimensional joint bound. The existing **1 MPa hot shear
estimate is not a normal/peel allowable**. No actual hot normal strength,
adhesive wetting or installed joint stiffness is inferred.

The 0.606381 mm isolated-panel estimate and 1 mm total service target leave
0.392422 mm for other foundation motion after the 0.1 mm clipped spring motion,
conditional on the separate estimates being compatible. The 0.3 mm case leaves
0.390322 mm. Neither remainder is an installed pass or a substitute for the
actual coupled compliance matrix. Existing unsupported axial component,
regional-stress convergence and dynamic flutter limitations remain open.

## Verification and resources

Before applying the clipping, every 16/32/64-point full-foot matrix, tab matrix
and common-root motion reproduced the archived common-root calculation with
zero reported difference at stored precision. A separate planar spring fixture
matches its analytic matrix to 1.91e-16 relative. Slot-clipped area quadrature
matches its independent analytic area within 2.3e-13 mm²; the final 32→64 root
motion changes are 6.30e-20 and 1.79e-15 relative for the 0.1/0.3 mm cases.
Maximum combined force/moment residual is 3.56e-14 in the recorded N/N·m
components. Quadrature convergence is not physical or spatial FEA convergence.

The single completed child used 0.271 CPU s including startup and 0.303 s wall,
with reported peak RSS 49,643,520 bytes. One worker and one-thread environment
were requested; these elapsed observations do not by themselves prove all
library threads ran on only one core. There was no retry or new CAD, FEA,
thermal or flight run. The record and completion evidence are in
`build/wo03-clipped-foot-contact-v1/`; source/config hashes were recorded before
and after and remained unchanged. Exact driver, config and finite-contact helper
copies were verified against those hashes and archived under
`executed-source-snapshot/`. A separate post-run comparison matched all
nine current CAD/schema/material source hashes to those recorded by the prior
face audit; files not captured both before and after the clipped worker are not
asserted immutable during it. Black, Ruff and the focused analytic spring-loss
test passed.
