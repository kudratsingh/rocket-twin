# G80-F1 construction and recovery source packet

**Source-intake state, 2026-11-02 local:** G80-F1 is a new, provisional fixed-motor G80 construction direction. The current manufacturer-linked Graduator and T-LOC instructions support a simple slotted body, bonded centering-ring/motor tube, bonded fin, forward-ring recovery-eye topology. They do **not** size or qualify G80-F1, prove its installed load path, establish an exact SKU 78010/retainer fit, or justify reducing the saved severe recovery demand. No G80-F1 numerical packet, CAD, FEA, simulator run, physical build or flight acceptance occurred here. A1 and the older C1/A/B arrangements stay frozen; G54 and G12 remain separate tracks.

Both are LOC construction practices, not independent capacity demonstrations. They suggest a much simpler **candidate** body/motor/fin/recovery arrangement than A1's removable aft joint, but G80-F1 must still have a complete body, fin, motor, positive retention, upper/nose and recovery load path. The manuals do not establish the body or fin stock material grade for a new cut build, the exact shear/bend properties, or finished epoxy bond strength. The selected **G80-F1 concept geometry** is a standard paper tube with 66.675 mm OD and 64.77 mm ID, a fixed 29 mm paper motor mount with two nominal 6 mm birch rings, a custom 160 mm nose and 760 mm body, and three nominal 6 mm birch through-the-wall fins (130 mm root, 65 mm tip, 60 mm span, 45 mm sweep; face grain intended spanwise). Its recovery concept is a forward motor-ring anchor, Kevlar heat leader and nylon shock cord, with no aft service joint. These values identify the candidate to be screened; they are not dimensions or approvals from either LOC manual. It does not have to copy the G12 physical upper/aft interface. Its own full mass, CG/CP, guide, fin/flutter, thermal, occupancy and recovery verification are still required.

The [AeroTech Initiator current product page](https://aerotech-rocketry.com/products/product_6884e3bb-9375-a3f8-ab43-42d8a31ec4ee) is a separate compatibility example: a 39 in × 2.6 in rocket with a 29 mm mount, molded Fin-Lok polymer fins, Labyrinth baffle, nylon chute and screw-on **aluminum** retainer, and it explicitly lists G80-10T among its suggested motors. Its 14 oz catalogue weight and modular molded joints cannot be transferred to a built G80-F1. An official current assembly PDF was not found in the bounded search.

### Birch stock and temperature gap

The final source was the [Metsä Wood Birch manufacturer data-sheet collection](https://www.metsagroup.com/contentassets/8a5519dc6e534fcfa99c06759fbb8bad/metsa_wood_birch_plywood_product_datasheets_en.pdf) (its standard Birch sheet is dated 02/2023). It identifies cross-bonded 1.4 mm birch veneers with phenol-formaldehyde bond, a **6.5 mm nominal five-ply stock** with 6.1–6.9 mm thickness range and 4.4 kg/m² sheet mass at stated moisture conditions, and EN 314-2 Class 3 bond classification. It states that a separate Declaration of Performance supplies design properties and that veneer orientation can change stiffness and strength. **6.5 mm is not the selected 6 mm fin/ring stock**, and the checked sheet supplies neither directional bending/planar-shear values for selected stock nor a 45 °C or motor-proximate derating. Its 4.4 kg/m² is not a cut-and-finished three-fin/ring mass. No thin-stock directional property or hot allowable was established within the source cap. Face-grain spanwise orientation remains a construction hypothesis; a selected 6 mm product, its DoP/test data, and hot/conditioned properties must precede any fin or ring capacity claim.

## Motor mount and retention evidence boundary

The existing [A2 supplier register](wo03-a2-supplier-evidence.md) already establishes the current AeroTech G80-10T **SKU 78010** sales identity: polymer case/bulkhead/nozzle, nominal 29 mm diameter and 124 mm *casing* length. It does not provide a controlled thrust-ring, case, forward end, nozzle or full assembled length drawing. The 2007 phenolic 1.25 in ring and the NAR family 29 × 128 mm certification envelope are different kinds of evidence; neither is a current 78010 mating drawing. The former A1 seat at z1193–1195 intersects its retained liner/foil at z1072–1200 and is not inherited by G80-F1.

The [LOC 29 mm motor tube page](https://locprecision.com/products/motor-mount-tubing/) offers 6 in and 12 in lengths, without controlled ID/OD/tolerance or a cut-part mass. The [LOC PRO retainer page](https://locprecision.com/products/loc-pro-motor-retainer) says its LOC-tube 29 mm sleeve ID is 30.1 mm and directs epoxy installation on the aft tube. It does not give a motor contact/seat drawing, permissible tube variation, cap thread/lip engagement, hot/reverse load or SKU 78010 approval. Its PML-specific variant has a different sleeve size, so nominal 29 mm is not sufficient. An [Apogee comparison chart](https://www.apogeerockets.com/downloads/PDFs/Screw-on-Retainer-Chart.pdf) lists its Estes 29 mm resale item 24020 with approximate body ID 1.220 in, tube insertion depth 0.455 in, body OD 1.460 in, cap OD 1.744 in, cap height 0.741 in and assembled height 0.861 in. This older **third-party** chart is not an Estes 009750 revision-controlled drawing or evidence of current 78010 approval. Chart mass is not installed retainer/adhesive mass. The existing [Estes 009750 page](https://estesrockets.com/products/pro-series-ii-29-mm-motor-retainer-set) says heat-resistant ABS and “most” 29 mm composite motors, with no explicit 78010 pairing. No complete commercial tube + retainer + current motor stack was established.

Before a G80-F1 motor/retainer configuration can be drawn as fitted, obtain the intended current 78010 ring/shoulder diameter, axial thickness and datum, full case and nozzle sweep, hot and cold envelope; the chosen mount tube's ID/OD and tolerance; and the retainer's sleeve, lip, cap, engagement, tube-overhang and approved motor-contact geometry, installation method and thermal/reverse-load bounds. A specific stocked combination and measured finished mass must then be entered into the full ledger. The current 78010 nominal OD cannot be treated as an interference or clearance proof.

## Recovery load origin and remaining work

The prior [A1/A2 allocation record](wo03-a2-mass-allocation.md) gives **2267.985132 N nominal and 2310.189531 N adverse** severe full-vehicle recovery demands, already factored once by DF3. They are historical A1-configuration demands, **not** a calculated G80-F1 demand, but no evidence here justifies reducing a carried severe case. A new G80-F1 deployment case must explicitly identify dry/burnout mass, chosen parachute's measured or warranted drag area, ejection delay and speed at deployment (early, near-apogee and credible late), line slack and stretch, nose/upper/aft separation order, and every load-bearing termination. The present 20.4 m/s and 0.3 m severe late probe is a prior assumption, not a proven G80-F1 event.

[RocketPy's documented simplified opening-shock utility](https://docs.rocketpy.org/en/develop/reference/classes/utils/utilities.html) describes a Knacke-style inflation estimate using parachute drag area, dynamic pressure and empirical opening coefficient, typically 1.2–2.0; it does not by itself resolve line-stretch/snatch or this vehicle's actual speed. In an [original NASA instrumented parachute flight test](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19670022936/downloads/19670022936.pdf), deployment, line stretch and inflation produced distinguishable riser-force peaks. That test used a full-size parachute and cannot supply G80-F1 numeric loads. The [NASA NESC soft-goods report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20100040275/downloads/20100040275.pdf?attachment=true) explains element-level load allocation and strength losses from seams, wear, temperature and asymmetric loading; its human-rated design factors are not imported into this project's DF3. An [Apogee rocket recovery case study](https://www.apogeerockets.com/education/downloads/Newsletter348.pdf) describes late ejection and elastic stretch as important, but its example is not a capacity test or a portable reduction factor.

The two LOC manuals assume their supplied chute, fire blanket, cord/eye and nose arrangements for the corresponding kits. They publish no opening-speed limit, allowable snatch load, finished eye or knot strength, conditioned line capacity, epoxy/centering-ring/body joint capacity, or full-vehicle recovery force. For G80-F1, a manufacturer's **cord-only** tensile rating cannot be assigned to the sewn/knotted eye, bend, screw eye or installed paper-body joint. A lighter 29 mm fixed build could change the severe demand, but it needs a complete configuration-specific recovery calculation with early/late sensitivity and then separate installed path proof. No load reduction or allocation is made by this source packet.
