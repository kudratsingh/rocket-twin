## Madcow: tube loop, payload eye and an incompletely specified nose connection

In the [manufacturer manual](https://www.madcowrocketry.com/content/pdf/dx3.pdf),
p2 Steps1–3, a secured Kevlar loop seats against the notched forward motor-tube
ring and joins nylon; rings bond the mount into the body. Page3 Steps4/6 bond
fin tangs to tube/body and a payload-eye bulkhead/coupler into the payload tube.
Page4 Step8 connects cord/chute at that eye, protects the canopy and requires
positive motor retention.

```text
body ← bonded rings / motor tube ← Kevlar loop ← nylon → payload bulkhead eye
                                                    → canopy
payload bulkhead → bonded coupler → payload tube → nose retention unspecified
```

Short source quote, **p2 Step2**: “Wrap the end of the Kevlar shock cord around
the forward end of the motor tube”. The same step calls the far attachment
the nose, whereas **p4 Step8** identifies the payload eye. No explicit
nose-to-payload fastening or later anchor-replacement procedure was found.
Official total cord length and elasticity are unstated. This is an incomplete
nose/body definition.

## LOC: forward-ring eye and separately retained payload/nose

The [current linked manual](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0568/7489/3503/files/PK-56_HiTech_Build_Instructions.pdf?v=1639515959)
uses a forward-ring eyebolt with two nuts/washers; three rings bond to the
motor tube (**p1 Steps1–2**). Sewn-loop tubular nylon hitches to the eye;
mount/rings and fins bond into the booster (**p2 Steps3–5**). A payload screw
eye/bulkhead/coupler bonds into the payload tube; cord passes through a blanket,
knots at that eye with a3 ft tail, then connects to the chute (**p3 Steps6–9**).
**P4 Step10** retains the nose using tape or optional rivets/screws: “Make sure
the cone will NOT come off”. Total cord length, extension and later anchor
replacement are unspecified.

```text
body ← ring/body bonds ← forward ring + eye ← sewn-loop nylon → payload screw eye
                                                           → canopy
payload eye → bulkhead/coupler bonds → payload tube → retained nose
motor → separate retainer / motor tube → rings/body
```

The [current storefront](https://locprecision.com/products/hi-tech) instead
describes a **Kevlar mount plus nylon webbing**. The linked manual's parts list
says3/16 in tubular nylon and its steps attach it directly to the eye. This
is an unresolved product/instruction difference, not permission to combine
both into a single claimed stock construction. Nylon material alone establishes
no bungee behavior, arrest travel, damping or finished strength.

An older [manufacturer-authored PK-56 manual, ©2013, hosted by Apogee](https://www.apogeerockets.com/downloads/instructions/PK-56_Hi-Tech_Assy.pdf)
has **two rings and an epoxy-covered knotted cord mount on the inner body wall**,
plus a nylon-elastic main cord (**p2, Shock Cord Mount Instructions / parts list**).
It also discusses payload-eye and nose retention. This is a historical version,
not the current five-page eye/ring assembly. The old text contains conflicting
nose-versus-payload attachment wording; do not stitch its steps into the newer
manual. It supplies no finished joint load rating.

## Firsthand reports and failure attribution

The [manufacturer-linked Mike Caplinger DX3 review](https://www.rocketreviews.com/madcow-rocketry-dx3-by-mike-caplinger.html)
is **firsthand narrative**, not a strength test. Its direct page returned403;
the search index exposed the article text, so this has an explicit access
limitation. It reports a finished18.2 oz build versus15 oz advertised, without
a clear loaded/empty label; about8 ft cord in the parts discussion; four
E30-4T flights with two nose separations despite tape, and section-contact
marks. His suggestion that longer cord might help is opinion, not a measured
cause or an approved correction. No G80 recovery-force result is reported.

The [current Madcow page's Cory Antosh review](https://madcowrocketry.com/2-6-dx3/),
dated2025-05-28, reports repeated flights and a G77R flight. This is another
user account, not manufacturer certification or a force measurement.

A [Mike Terry Hi-Tech account](https://www.rocketreviews.com/mike-terrys-page.html)
was likewise403 on direct access. Its indexed first-person text reports a
zipper after descending deployment on an H123 and rebuilding with a longer
tube. Exact kit revision, harness, modifications and event force are unknown;
it is a historical family anecdote, not an established failure of today's
PK-56 ring-eye assembly.

Failure search also exposed the [2001-06-16 Tripoli Montana firsthand launch
report](https://tripolimontana.org/demery/Reports/Launch20010616.html). Reading
the whole paragraph establishes that its cord/parachute tangle concerned the
**Arcas**, not the Hi-Tech. It reports a satisfactory Hi-Tech flight. This
prevents a misleading search-snippet attribution; it is not a Hi-Tech
durability test. No directly verified exact-current-PK-56 attachment failure
was established in this bounded search, and no reliability claim is inferred
from that absence.
