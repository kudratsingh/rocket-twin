# G80 captive retention: one pre-shell keeper proposal

Select one **4 mm G11 keeper ring** for a bounded geometry study, replacing the source rear **3 mm plywood ring** while retaining its aft face at local 170 mm. Load the nuts and washers into blind pockets from the aft face **before closing the keeper against the separate G11 bridge and inserting the mount into the shell**. The cap screws and motor can then be removed from the aft end while the nuts remain mechanically captured. The keeper's intended roles are nut anti-rotation and axial stowage; under reverse thrust the nuts and washers must bear **directly on the G11 bridge**, whose tabs transfer load to the rails. Neither the keeper nor elastic glue is credited as a reverse-load washer seat.

This is a proposed assembly sequence and geometry for one next bounded check, **not an executed third CAD attempt**, an adopted 52-part package or a retention pass. The earlier two attempts and their stopped/replan artifacts remain unchanged. If a nut or keeper fails after the shell is bonded, the proposed service condition is **remove or replace the aft module on the bench**, not remove the nut radially through the assembled shell. Whether replacement without destroying the shell is possible remains unverified.

## Existing evidence and the assembly distinction

The first [retention geometry attempt](wo03-g80-retention-geometry.md) found each nominal 9 mm washer intersected the mount by **1.098419 mm³** because the source relief began at R21.5 while the washer reached R21.0. The one authorized replan started the relief at R20.7 with 9.6 mm nominal width: screened nominal solids cleared, and the three bridge washer probes each measured **54.538 mm²**. All three sampled straight radial nut/washer removal paths nevertheless encountered the installed shell; no keeper was modeled. This blocks **that removal path with the shell installed**. It does not establish that the cap and motor need nut removal, or that pre-shell assembly is impossible. Neither attempt proves a tolerance fit, positive capture, access or a rated connection.

The source set has a 9.0 mm nominal washer OD and 3.2–3.38 mm ID; washer OD tolerance is unavailable. The source hardware YAML specifies **0.7–0.9 mm washer thickness** and a **5.32–5.5 mm across-flats, 2.15–2.4 mm high nut**. The supplier and companion hardware table give +0/−0.2 mm from 0.9 mm, consistently 0.7–0.9 mm. An earlier draft of this plan incorrectly described a ±0.2 mm discrepancy; no such discrepancy is established. The 1.1 mm counterbore depth below is a proposed pocket dimension, not a washer thickness or supplier tolerance. Unknown maximum OD and other tolerances still preclude a manufacturing pass. The supplier listings' orderability and fastener property classes are not established. [Hardware evidence](wo03-g80-retention-hardware.md).

## Selected keeper and closure geometry

All dimensions are provisional, in millimetres local to the mount's 1.010 m station. The [study configuration](../configs/studies/wo03-g80-captive-retention-plan.yaml) owns them; no source CAD has been changed.

- Use one G11 annulus **R15.8–R32.2**, local **z=166–170**, retaining the original rear-ring aft face at z=170. Keep the three existing rail and fin-channel positions. The separate 2 mm G11 bridge begins at z=170.1; retain the nominal **0.1 mm** keeper/bridge gap and the three M3 axes at R25.5, angles 60°/180°/300°.
- At each axis, open a **9.6 mm diameter washer counterbore** from the keeper's aft face to depth **1.1 mm**, then a **5.7 mm across-flats hex nut pocket** down to local z=166.5. The remaining **0.5 mm forward wall** stops the nut falling forward; a **3.6 mm through-bore** in that wall permits a nominal 3 mm screw tip to project past the nut. The bridge's own 3.4 mm bore remains separately specified. The screw-tip path must be checked through the actual front-side assembly, including the 35 mm source screw's nominal protrusion.
- A 5.7 mm hex pocket offers **0.1 mm nominal clearance per flat** for a 5.5 mm maximum-AF nut. The calculated 5.5 mm hex corner diameter is approximately **6.351 mm**; the ideal 5.7 mm pocket's corner diameter is approximately 6.582 mm. Propose six **1.0 mm diameter corner-relief cuts** to permit a round milling tool to clear the hex corners; confirm machining depth, fillets and actual nut engagement. This is a machining concept, not an as-built fit or a verified anti-rotation torque rating.
- The washer sits directly against the bridge forward face, not on the keeper's shoulder. With the 0.1 mm keeper/bridge gap, z=166.5 pocket floor and z=170.1 bridge face provide **3.6 mm** nominal axial space: the source maximum 0.9 + 2.4 = **3.3 mm** stack leaves **0.3 mm** nominal axial freedom. These are arithmetic clearances before machining, alignment, bolt, bridge and washer-OD tolerances. For the source minimum 0.7 mm washer and 2.15 mm nut, only about **1.65 mm of nut height** lies inside the nominal hex depth; that engagement is unqualified.
- To close the keeper mechanically while the three main cap bolts are removed, propose **three additional M2 stowage fasteners** through nominal **2.2 mm holes** at R19.0, angles 30°/150°/270°, through keeper and bridge. Provide one nominal **4.0 mm OD × 2.2 mm ID × 0.1 mm standoff shim** around each M2 hole between keeper and bridge so tightening does not close the intended gap; actual shim and fastener tolerances, seating and compression must be checked. Their heads, nuts/locking, lengths, assembly tool access, actual hardware and shim mass and interference are unspecified. Their purpose is closure/stowage, not the credited reverse thrust path. No glued-only nut capture is assumed. Any unforeseen operating load or preload on these fasteners must be checked.

At nominal dimensions, the 9.6 mm washer pocket centered at R25.5 leaves **1.9 mm** to the keeper OD and **4.9 mm** to its bore; a 2.2 mm stowage hole centered at R19 leaves **2.1 mm** to the bore. These are isolated geometric differences, not ligaments after rail slots, adjacent pockets, hole tolerances, corner relief or machining damage. Exact remaining material, finite front wall, thermal expansion, keeper/bridge flatness, local bending and fastener pull-through are open. The source replan's radial-open 9.6 mm slots are replaced by **closed blind pockets**; do not assume their previous interference result applies to this different ring.

## Load path, service and mass

The intended reverse path remains motor aft shoulder → cap → three longer M3 screws → nuts and washers → **G11 bridge** → its six rail-flank tabs → rails/rings/shell and forward joint. The nut pocket prevents nut spin and the front wall/closure prevent dropout during service; the keeper is not an assumed structural substitute for the bridge. Direct bridge bearing under the washer and adequate screw engagement, preload, washer bending, nut stripping, G11 pull-through, bridge bending, tab adhesion/peel and shell load sharing all remain unqualified. Verify the source 150 N reverse case and any separately justified additional inertia load with the existing DF3; no strength rating is inferred from pocket geometry.

Proposed service sequence: machine the ring and pockets from sheet with appropriate G11 dust control; inspect dimensions and a trial nut's rotation; insert nuts and washers from aft; mechanically close keeper to bridge; verify each washer remains free to seat on the bridge and the screw-tip bores remain open; assemble the mount and shell; then insert/remove the three main screws and cap from aft for motor service. Nut installation, shell sliding, flange, rail/fin geometry, screw access and actual retention have **not** been geometrically verified. Straight radial nut replacement after shell bonding remains blocked in the previous sample; no claim is made for another route.

The uncut 4 mm R15.8–R32.2 G11 annular stock volume is **9,892.247 mm³**, or **18.301 g** at the existing estimated 1850 kg/m³. For comparison only, the uncut 3 mm plywood annulus at 715 kg/m³ is **5.305 g**, a **12.996 g gross-stock difference**. These are not the net replacement mass: each actual ring has pockets, slots, bores, joints and overlaps; the old ring's isolated saved solid and the proposed new ring do not have verified equivalent net volumes. The replan's 44.5075 g mount and 10.2190 g bridge are for different geometry and cannot be reused as the new package totals. Recompute actual parts, adhesives, full mass, CG and inertia before affected flight/static checks.

The source supplier's **8.52 g** is the nominal average for three candidate M3 screw/nut/washer stacks, not an increment. The existing **7 g** retention hardware allowance is unitemized: do not sum those as two physical installed M3 sets or claim the 1.52 g difference as a verified delta. Itemize the replacement stack and credit only demonstrably removed mass; add all three new M2 closure fasteners, heads/nuts/washers and 0.1 mm standoffs, whose mass is **unknown**. Preserve the separate **4.620 g annular upper budget**, **12 g finish allowance** and full **50 g growth allowance** until the complete nonoverlapping ledger is reconciled.

## Decision and one next check

This route is the selected **study direction**, conditional on whether the following single bounded geometry/known-case check can pass: on a fresh copy, generate the 4 mm keeper with its three stepped blind pockets, through-bores and three mechanical closure fasteners with standoffs; retain the prior bridge, actual shell/rails/fins and source M3 axes. First validate pocket/contact probes on a known flat blind-pocket fixture; then verify all three installed nut/washer envelopes, direct bridge bearing, screw protrusion, keeper wall, rail/fin clearances, cap/motor aft access and the **pre-shell assembly sequence**. Check tolerance intervals where source bounds exist, and mark washer OD, actual M2 hardware and assembly access **unavailable** where they do not. Enforce one CPU requested, a 120 s child limit and the project's 600 CPU s packet limit; stop on the first invalid geometry, unknown interference or cap, with no automatic retries.

If the 0.5 mm wall cannot be made reliably, a washer's actual OD cannot fit, the hex pocket cannot hold the nut, the keeper fasteners collide with rails/fins, the bridge cannot close after nut insertion, or the shell cannot be assembled over the closed hardware, this route remains blocked at that specific constraint. Source G11 directional/hot stiffness, ring-to-rail bonds, altered heat paths, strength, tolerance and physical verification remain separate open gates. The primary retains design selection and adoption; no new CAD, structural/thermal solver, flight run or physical work was performed for this plan.
