# WO-03 G80 capture material bounds

**Purpose.** Bound the evidence available for rail-aligned mechanical capture (B) versus a continuous captured G11 frame (C). This is a source audit and screening calculation, not a selected design, capacity finding, or material qualification. All strength arithmetic below is an ideal uniform-stress screen; it does not establish bearing, tear-out, joint, or support capacity.

## Screening arithmetic (not capacity)

For a uniform direct-stress illustration only, `A = F / σ`:

| Load path illustration | Screen used | Ideal area for 450 N retention | Ideal area for 1,782.253 N recovery | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| One plywood rail, generic weak-direction estimate | 10 MPa | 45 mm² | 178.2 mm² | The 450 N screen is below the rail’s 84 mm² gross area, but this says nothing about hole loss, bearing, edge tear-out, compression, support bending, or the true load split. Recovery exceeds one rail’s gross area even before those losses. |
| One plywood rail, fin-study 50% hot sensitivity applied to the 10 MPa estimate | 5 MPa | 90 mm² | 356.5 mm² | This is only a sensitivity illustration. At 450 N, 90 mm² exceeds the 84 mm² gross section of a single rail, so B cannot rely on one unmodified rail carrying the entire factored retention force under this sensitivity. It must demonstrate a different effective section/load route or a measured, justified distribution; equal sharing is not assumed. |
| G11 continuous member, project hot-strength estimate | 25 MPa | 18 mm² | 71.3 mm² | Ideal direct stress only. This does not qualify G11 bearing, edge breakout, holes, joints, buckling, bending, temperature history, or a real frame shape. The supplier’s 350 MPa hot flexural result is not substituted as a tensile/bearing allowable. |
| Paper shell, project generic estimate | 2 MPa | 225 mm² | 891.1 mm² | Both exceed the current gross shell annulus of 102.9 mm². This is a coarse comparison only; shell load direction, local crush/buckling, seams, and attachment are unqualified. Do not route either factored demand through the tube on this estimate. |

The estimates expose a limiting question for option B: where does the complete force enter and leave the capture, and what verified effective rail/ring section and support stiffness carry it? The current single-rail gross area is 84 mm², smaller than the 90 mm² direct-stress area under the illustrative 5 MPa hot sensitivity for 450 N. This is not a rejection criterion or allowable; it shows why a single-rail assumption cannot close the case. Option C also remains open until actual G11 directional net-section, bearing, buckling, attachment, and temperature behavior are bounded. Using the estimated 25 MPa G11 hot strength and 1,850 kg/m³ density, the ideal 71.3 mm² recovery section has about 131.9 g of mass per metre of continuous member before cutouts, joints, geometry, and hardware. This illustrates the aft-mass/CG trade; it is not a proposed member size or mass estimate for either candidate.

## B versus C evidence boundary

| Prospective path | Potential load-path change | Current limiting evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **B — rail-aligned mechanical capture** | Align capture reactions with the three structural plywood rails and use positive G11 bearing/capture so hot adhesive is not the sole reverse-load path. This may preserve the light wood frame where its actual geometry and joints are supported by evidence. | Retainer axes currently fall between rails; the route from each retainer to pocket flanks, rings, rails, and body is not a measured or solved flexible-support load path. The 84 mm² gross rail section and estimated properties do not prove bearing, tear-out, or hot strength. Must not rely on three-rail equal sharing. A support stiffness/deflection bound and mode-specific joint/rail tests are needed. |
| **C — continuous captured G11 frame/cage** | A continuous mechanically captured load frame could bypass the adhesive-only plywood path and provide a more direct load route. | Actual G11 grade/form/lamination axes, hot directional tensile/compressive/shear and bearing behavior, net section, local buckling, connections, and support flexibility remain open. The project 25 MPa hot value only gives a notional direct-stress area. Any aft mass and geometry change must be added once to the complete candidate ledger and checked for CG, guide interaction, access, recovery routing, and performance. This is a future G80 candidate distinction, not a change to the 72-part baseline or the separate 73-part canopy hypothesis. |

Neither path is accepted by this audit. Do not use prohibited metal-body structural load rods or treat a general tensile/flexural value as pin-bearing capacity. Existing bridge stress/reaction evidence is numerical demand information with known limitations; it is not a qualification of real rail supports or material joints.

## Concrete evidence needed to advance either path

Before a physical capacity claim, close these exact inputs for the selected geometry rather than beginning a broad materials study:

1. **Plywood identity and axes:** exact supplier/SKU/grade, veneer count and thickness, sheet/lot certificate, actual thickness and moisture condition; mark face-grain and cross-ply axes on each ring and rail. Obtain mode-specific lot or representative coupon data for axial tension/compression, in-plane and edgewise shear, pin/bolt bearing, edge tear-out versus edge distance, and net section, including the project’s measured temperature and moisture conditioning. The current fabrication grain directions are proposed, not verified.
2. **G11 identity and modes:** exact supplier, grade, product form, lot, thickness, and lamination axes; obtain compression/bearing, pin bearing, edge breakout, net-section tension, shear, and flexural data in the actual axes and relevant temperature/dwell. Do not map the ambiguous BASFF compression endpoints or Atlas labels to an allowable without supplier clarification.
3. **Actual interface and support:** document the real pocket dimensions, fit/gap, surface preparation, adhesive identity/cure, bond coverage, and rail/ring assembly. Test representative plywood–G11 and plywood–paper joints in the relevant bearing, shear, peel, and mixed modes at the measured hot condition. Bound support deflection/stiffness and reaction distribution for the actual ring/rail/body attachment; do not assume equal force among rails.
4. **Paper only if it remains in the load path:** identify the exact tube construction and orientation and establish hot/moist bearing, compression, local buckling, seam, and attachment behavior. The generic 2 MPa screen is insufficient for either factored load case.
5. **Temperature history:** provide the actual case-specific material/interface temperature history and dwell for retention and recovery. The 45°C matrix value is a screen; Tg and a process-temperature statement do not substitute for hot joint or substrate data.

Until those inputs exist, B’s concrete limitation is the unverified rail/ring/body load route and the fact that the 84 mm² single-rail gross section is already below the 90 mm² ideal area from the illustrative 5 MPa/450 N sensitivity. C’s concrete limitation is the absence of mode- and direction-specific hot G11 allowables and a supportable geometry/mass ledger. The present evidence can guide the next bounded geometry comparison and specimen definition, but cannot establish capacity or select hardware.
