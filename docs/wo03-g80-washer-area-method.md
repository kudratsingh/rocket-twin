# G80 washer area: independent quadratic-boundary known case

The known annulus meets the existing **0.0002 mm² absolute area criterion** at the finest of three predeclared boundary resolutions. This is evidence about the representation of circular edges on a small planar mesh; it is not an actual bridge mesh, a solver result, or a structural acceptance.

The independently specified annulus has outer radius 4.5 mm and inner radius 1.7 mm, for exact area **54.538048466319 mm²**. Each case used Gmsh quadratic triangles, Gauss6 surface integration, and a requested single worker, with interior maximum size 1.5 mm. The [plan](../build/wo03-g80-washer-area-known-v1/plan.json) fixed all three boundary segment counts before execution, a 10 s CPU and wall cap per case, and a 20 CPU s total packet cap. No 3D mesh or CalculiX case ran.

The mesh centroids were within 2.3e-16 mm of the exact origin. These symmetric known cases do not reproduce or bound the actual bridge patch's mesh-centroid shift. Each boundary's quadratic midside lies on its circle; between these nodes it is a quadratic arc, not an exact circular arc. An independent Green-theorem calculation gives the area of an n-segment, radius-r quadratic circle as `n r² [(4/3) sin(π/n) − (1/6) sin(2π/n)]`. Applying that formula separately to the outer and inner edges reproduced the integrated mesh areas to within **1.7e-13 mm²**. At the coarsest level, the outer quadratic circle underestimates its exact disk by 0.000621330 mm² while the more coarsely segmented inner circle underestimates its disk by 0.007066291 mm². Since annulus area subtracts the inner disk, the larger inner deficit produces the positive annulus error. The same relationship holds at the other two levels.

The agreement with the boundary-only analytic area identifies boundary geometry as the controlling area approximation **for these conforming planar meshes**. The study did not run a separate interior-only mesh refinement and does not establish the exact segment count or feasibility of a future 3D bridge mesh. Any future surface method must still meet the existing guard on the actual clipped patch and satisfy all other topology, load, resource and equilibrium checks; these results authorize no additional attempt or changed tolerance.

The three child processes used **0.331579 CPU s total** and 0.407 s aggregate wall time, with observed process peak RSS between 57.7 and 63.6 MB. The [case script](../build/wo03-g80-washer-area-known-v1/case.py), [runner](../build/wo03-g80-washer-area-known-v1/run.py), [progress](../build/wo03-g80-washer-area-known-v1/progress.json) and [results](../build/wo03-g80-washer-area-known-v1/result.json) preserve the planned and actual cases. The adapter used for Gauss6 integration had SHA-256 `1aeed6a5abf07c904bb5fcf44e4e06b7ec5fa4ac5aea9d9288085390d88192f1` at post-run inspection; no production adapter or earlier source record was edited in this diagnostic.

## Proposed boundary-control helper, small cases only

The separate [boundary helper](../scripts/wo03_g80_washer_boundary.py) and [study configuration](../configs/studies/wo03-g80-washer-boundary.yaml) specify **96 outer and 32 inner segments per full-circle equivalent**, retaining the existing 1.5 mm bulk target and 50,000-node, 75,000-tet and 2 GiB guards. The helper applies no mesh or solver itself. It receives already-identified patch faces and original source STEP washer-face centers, obtains their combined exterior curves, and requires each curve to have Gmsh type `Circle`, match the relevant source-centered 4.5 mm outer or **1.7 mm patch-hole** radius and plane, and have a supported angular parameter range and arc length. It requires a complete, nonoverlapping inner and outer circle; unknown curves, unsupported partial rings, conflicting shared-curve assignments and source-hash mismatches stop before sizing. For split arcs it assigns `ceil(full_circle_segments × arc_length / circumference)` elements per existing curve; existing shared Gmsh curve tags remain shared. Other curves are not assigned by this helper.

The center source is the original washer STEP face, whose three SHA-256 values are explicitly pinned in the configuration alongside the bridge STEP. Read-only imports confirmed both boundary curves of each original washer face have Gmsh type `Circle`; their radii are **4.5 and 1.6 mm**. The proposed **1.7 mm inner patch boundary** is the bridge-hole/imprinted patch boundary, not the source washer's 1.6 mm hole. The future caller must verify its center against the pinned STEP and pass actual imprinted patch face tags; this helper does not claim those actual 3D fragmented edges have yet been identified or proven compatible. It rejects an otherwise geometrically circular curve represented by Gmsh as `Ellipse`, rather than assuming it is a supported `Circle`.

Five [small-case tests](../tests/test_g80_washer_boundary.py) passed: the 96/32 independent annulus meets the unchanged 0.0002 mm² area guard; measured boundary arc spacing and segment counts follow the selected distribution; an unrelated pre-sized circle retains its original 12 segments; unequal split circular arcs receive length-proportional counts; and wrong centers, noncircular boundaries, and changed source hashes are rejected. The initial fixture used `addDisk`, which Gmsh exposed as `Ellipse`; strict rejection was expected for that representation, so the fixture was changed to explicit `addCircle` geometry. The final five-test run used **0.483 CPU s, 0.441 s wall and 83,918,848 bytes peak RSS**, under the declared 10 CPU s and wall per fixture and 20 CPU s aggregate allowance. The three separate original washer STEP imports used 0.144 CPU s and no mesh. All fixture and read-only source checks remained below the packet ceiling.

No production bridge adapter or driver was edited for this proposal. No actual bridge remesh, CCX case, geometry adoption, area-tolerance change or strength conclusion followed. Integrating this helper with an actual fragmented bridge, validating ownership of every intended edge and applying the original resource/input guards require a separately authorized case.
