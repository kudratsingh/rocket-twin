# G80 recovery backbone trade — 2026-11-01

**Recommend a continuous aramid recovery backbone for the next geometry study**, with separate positive branch attachments to the nose, forward-body/payload carrier and aft structural mass. It must cross the 0.500 m paper-tube cut and the removable module joint without making the tube or the motor-retention bridge the only load path. This is a layout direction, not an accepted joint or proof of strength. The existing late-deployment 20.4 m/s probe is not an approved deployment limit, and the actual opening shock remains unknown.

The existing [recovery intake](wo03-g80-recovery-path-intake.md) gives **1,782.253 N** for its full-loaded, 0.3 m stopping-distance probe, including gravity and DF3 once. Use this entire already-factored demand as an intentionally severe screening load for each proposed primary backbone segment and its terminations; it is not a measured transient, a requirement to multiply by three again, or a guarantee that all branches see that load simultaneously. The [axial-cut screen](wo03-g80-recovery-axial-screen.md) finds only the cardboard `body_upper` crossing 0.500 m; its off-nominal gross stress lower bounds of 4.631–10.416 MPa exceed the estimated 2 MPa paper screen. A local forward spreader alone does not bypass this cut.

The G11 material numbers are the project's estimated `configs/engineering/wo03.yaml` screening profile, not a measured G80 laminate allowable. Its density/strength assumptions and 25 MPa illustrative sensitivity must not be treated as hot or bond allowables. For context only, [DuPont's Kevlar technical guide](https://www.dupont.com/content/dam/aramids/amer/us/en/safety/public/documents/en/Kevlar_Technical_Guide_0319.pdf) reports Kevlar 29 yarn density 1.44 g/cm³ and conditioned straight-yarn tensile strength about 2,920 MPa. Dividing 1,782.253 N by that yarn number gives an ideal **0.610 mm² fiber** lower bound (0.878 g/m of fiber); it is neither the cross-section nor allowable of a finished cord or knot. Cord braid geometry, knots, sewn loops, bend radius, chafe, UV, moisture, aging, hot gas and ejection protection require their own evidence. An aramid backbone may be relatively stiff: the 0.3/0.6 m stopping distances cannot be assumed to result from its presence, and snatch can increase loads.

The saved geometry has a G11 bulkhead at 0.225 m and a 3.4 mm nominal anchor hole, but its 4 g modeled anchor lacks a specified grade, thread, attachment or rating. Do not credit it, its thin-paper bond, or a new industrial lifting eye as the backbone joint. Develop a compact replaceable soft-loop or stitched/sleeved junction and load-spreading attachment around the forward bulkhead/carrier, with independent nose retention and a defined payload-carrier restraint; the current nose shoulder has no positive recovery anchor. For the aft end, design a dedicated positive attachment to the actual aft structural assembly and show how its finite joint transfers force to the shell, fins, mount **and retained motor**; no proposed aft member currently has verified capacity. Route and protect the tether through the real carrier/avionics envelopes and along the 64 mm coupler without obstructing independent recovery electronics, ejection, parachute packing, fin channels, motor/retainer access or module separation. A service loop or separable inspected connection at the joint must preserve a continuous load path when assembled and permit the aft module and motor to be serviced; its connector and load path are presently unspecified. A direct tether-to-canopy junction and separately retained nose avoid assuming a paper-tube glue joint transfers every load, but each restrained body and payload still needs its own credible load introduction.

**Next bounded check:** make one exact-identity layout of that tether and three attachment interfaces, with a continuous centerline/termination map across the module joint, swept packing/ejection and service clearances, and an itemized length/mass budget. At every branch and connector require an explicit force freebody up to the existing factored 1,782.253 N screening demand, without assuming equal sharing; identify realistic termination, hot exposure and load-spreader tests before assigning capacity. Stop if no protected route or removable aft attachment fits without crossing active avionics, tethering the ejecting canopy, or blocking motor removal. This check does not establish flight acceptance, body attachment strength, hot properties, canopy opening, thermal qualification or an actual proof load. The reinforced-body option is not selected now because it adds much more ideal solid mass and introduces long bonded-joint and removable-joint dependencies; it remains unqualified, not disproven. No active specification was changed and no CAD, solver, flight, purchase or physical test was performed.
